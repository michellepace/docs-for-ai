# Create and share a Team Bot

Make a Bot your whole team can use, then bring it to Slack or Microsoft Teams.
To learn how roles, memory, and usage work first, see
[Team Bots](/grok-bot/team-bots).

Team Bots need a Team or Enterprise plan and at least one other person on your
team. Team Bots run in the cloud, so a Bot that runs on your own computer stays
personal.

## Create a Team Bot

Everyone you share the Bot with can use what you add to it. Add only the apps,
secrets, skills, and files they may use.

1. Choose **+** in the sidebar, then **Create a Bot**, and give it a name.
2. Tell it what job it does for the team. A sentence is enough.
3. Follow its setup. It asks for plugins, the apps it connects to, secrets,
   skills, and files. Until you publish, it's a **Draft** that only you can
   see.
4. When the Bot says it's ready, choose **Publish**. Teammates can then find it
   under **Browse Team Bots**.

## Share it

1. Choose **Share** at the top right of the Bot's chat.
2. Under **Who has access**, choose your team, **Everyone in your
   organization**, or **People you invite**.
3. To invite someone, add them under **Add people or groups**.
4. To make someone a Manager, open their role and choose **Manager**.
5. To send a link, choose **Copy link**.

To share a Bot you already have, check its apps, secrets, skills, and files,
then choose **Share**. To stop sharing, choose **Only you**. Only the owner can
stop sharing.

## Find and use a Team Bot

1. Choose **+** in the sidebar, then **Browse Team Bots**. On desktop, you can
   also press `Cmd/Ctrl+K` and choose **Browse Team Bots**.
2. Choose **Add** to keep a Bot in your sidebar.

The list starts with **Popular** Bots, and **Team approved** marks Bots a team
admin approved. You can also ask one of your own Bots, like "Is there a Team
Bot for launch planning?", or open a link a teammate sent.

A Team Bot's link opens the same Bot for everyone.
[Sharing a Bot template](/grok-bot/bots#share-a-bot) gives each person a copy
of their own instead. You can voice chat with a Team Bot on desktop and add it
to group chats with other cloud Bots.

To make every Team Bot ask again before it uses your apps, choose **Clear
connector preferences** in **Settings** > **General** > **Team Bots**.

## Review what it learns

The owner and Managers review what Collaborators teach a Team Bot for the team.

1. Open the Bot's details and choose **Review learnings**. **Knowledge** opens.
2. Under **To review**, choose **Approve** or **Reject** for each suggestion.

Approved suggestions appear under **Recently learned**.

## Share one account with the team

A shared connector lets everyone use an app through one account. If you share
your own account, everyone who talks to the Bot acts through it, changes
included. Share only an account that holds nothing sensitive.

A team admin turns on **Allow Shared Logins** first. Then, as the owner or a
Manager:

1. Open the Bot's details and choose **Service accounts** under **Setup**.
2. Under **Shared connectors**, choose an app.
3. Choose **Use individual accounts** to have each teammate connect their own,
   or **Share your account** to have everyone act through yours.

Some apps also let the Bot sign in with its own account, a separate account for
the team. To stop, choose **Stop sharing** on the app's row, or remove the app.
Only the Manager whose account it is can reconnect it.

## Add it to Slack

Share the Bot before you connect it. Then, as the owner or a Manager:

1. Open the Bot's details and find **Bring to your team's Slack**.
2. Choose **Connect**. If it says **Awaiting admin approval**, ask a Slack
   workspace admin to approve the app.

Once it's **Connected**, teammates can message the Bot or mention it in
channels. Each teammate links their Slack account once per workspace, so the Bot
knows who they are: choose **Connect** when the Bot asks, or **Link** under
**Settings** > **General** > **Team Bots**. In a thread, the Bot acts for the
teammate who mentioned it, and can hand work to that person's own Bots.

Who can talk to it depends on who it's shared with:

* If it's shared with your organization, any full member of your Slack
  workspace can mention it, even without a linked account or a seat. They're
  told some features will be limited, and must link before a DM.
* If it's shared with your team or certain people, only those people can use it.
* A personal Bot only talks to its owner.

Slack guests, Slack Connect channels, and other workspaces can't use a Team Bot.

## Use it in Microsoft Teams

A team admin turns on Microsoft Teams first. Then the owner or a Manager
connects the Bot under **Bring to your team's Microsoft Teams**, and a
Microsoft Teams admin approves it. Teammates mention it in a channel, and it
answers under the post. For every step, see
[Use Grok Bot in Microsoft Teams](/grok-bot/microsoft-teams).

## Change or delete a Team Bot

The owner and Managers change a Team Bot from its details, on the right of its
chat: **Plugins**, **Secrets**, **Skills**, **Files**, and **About**. The wrench
icon opens **Manage**, with the Bot's access, channels, **Knowledge**, and
usage.

Deleting a Team Bot removes it, and everyone's chats with it, for the whole
team. Only the owner can delete one: right-click it in the sidebar and choose
**Delete**.

Admins control sharing, shared logins, and Slack access for people outside the
team on the Grok Bot page of the dashboard. See
[Grok Bot for teams and enterprises](/grok-bot/teams-and-enterprises#manage-bots).
If something doesn't work, see
[A Team Bot is missing or blocked](/grok-bot/troubleshooting#a-team-bot-is-missing-or-blocked).
