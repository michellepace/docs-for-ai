#### Use Grok Bot

# Team Bots

Team Bots let you share a Grok Bot with your team. Think of one like an
internal tool: build a [Bot](/grok-bot/bots) that handles a real workflow, then
make it available to a teammate or your whole team. They can use it in Grok
Bot, Slack, Microsoft Teams, or even [your calls](/grok-bot/meetings).

Anyone on your team can create a Team Bot, and it comes with permissions: only
its owner and Managers can change it.

Where a [Bot with a job](/grok-bot/use-cases#turn-tasks-you-repeat-into-a-bot)
takes on a task you repeat, a Team Bot takes it on for the whole team: a Launch
Bot that keeps a campaign on schedule, a Copy Bot that writes in your brand
voice, or a Research Bot that answers questions from customer interviews.

Team Bots need a Team or Enterprise plan and at least one other person on your
team. To make one, see
[Create and share a Team Bot](/grok-bot/create-a-team-bot).

## Where Team Bots work

Each teammate has a private chat with a Team Bot in Grok Bot, on desktop or
their phone. It also works where your team already talks. In Slack, mention it
in a channel or thread, or send it a DM.

In Microsoft Teams, mention it in a channel, add it to a group chat, or message
it.

To connect it, see [Add it to Slack](/grok-bot/create-a-team-bot#add-it-to-slack)
and [Use it in Microsoft Teams](/grok-bot/create-a-team-bot#use-it-in-microsoft-teams).

A Team Bot can also join Google Meet, Zoom, and Microsoft Teams calls when your
team allows meetings. See [Add a Bot to your meetings](/grok-bot/meetings).

## How a Team Bot works

Everyone works with the same Bot, with one set of apps, secrets, skills, and
files. Each person's chat with it is private: the owner and Managers can't read
yours, and you can't read theirs.

### Who can do what

Anyone on your team can create and share a Team Bot, unless a team admin limits
sharing to admins. A Bot can have any number of Managers, so the whole team can
look after it.

| Role | What they can do |
| --- | --- |
| Owner | Everything a Manager can, and delete the Bot. The owner is whoever created it. |
| Manager | Change the Bot's settings, review what it learns, and share it. |
| Collaborator | Chat with the Bot and teach it. |

### What it learns

A Team Bot learns from everyone who works with it: the team's decisions,
preferences, and better ways to do a task. Team memory and skills apply to
everyone, so when one person teaches it something, the whole team benefits.

Shared memory is why learning has a reviewer: one person's lesson changes the
Bot for everyone. When a Collaborator teaches it something for the team, the
Bot saves a suggestion, and the owner or a Manager approves or rejects it.
Changes the owner or a Manager makes themselves apply right away. What the Bot
learns about you stays in your own chats. See [Review what it learns](/grok-bot/create-a-team-bot#review-what-it-learns).

### Whose apps it uses

A Team Bot uses the apps and secrets its owner added. In your own chat, it can
also use your apps, but asks first with an **Allow** card. In a shared chat, it
uses its owner's apps and asks before it uses yours. Shared chats are Slack
channels, threads, and group DMs, and group chats in Grok Bot.

Your account only ever acts for you. The one exception is a shared connector: a
Manager lets everyone use an app through one account. That saves each teammate
from connecting the app, but anyone who talks to the Bot can then act as that
account, changes included. Share only an account that holds nothing sensitive.
See [Share one account with the team](/grok-bot/create-a-team-bot#share-one-account-with-the-team).

### Whose computer and usage

| | Your own chat, or a Slack DM | A shared chat |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Computer | Your Grok Bot computer | One shared computer of the Bot's own |
| Usage | Yours | The person it's answering |
| Who answers approval cards | You | Only the person it's answering |

In a shared chat, the Bot never works on your computer, so nothing on it shows
up in a channel. A reply in Slack to someone who hasn't linked an account, or
isn't on your team, uses your team's usage.

## Best practices

* Give each Team Bot one job, and name it for that job: a Launch Bot, a Copy
  Bot, a Research Bot.
* Add only the apps, secrets, and files that everyone you share it with may use.
* Add a second Manager, so suggestions don't wait on one person.
* Share an account only when it holds nothing sensitive.
